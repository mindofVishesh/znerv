ZNERV’s economic constraints include a total budget of $750, which will primarily fund hardware because our project includes a wearable spinal electrical stimulation device alongside its software. Stimulation electronics, an embedded controller, electrodes, and an enclosure must fit within this budget. We will prioritize free software development tools to preserve funding for the physical prototype. Professional constraints apply because our team must satisfy both Computer Science and Biomedical Engineering senior design requirements. Our team contract assigns Akshit Gupte biomedical requirements and document control, Vishesh Parwani system design and frontend development, and Aarohi Shah backend development, requiring coordinated integration and documented interfaces. Since we have no dedicated Electrical Engineering member, stimulation-circuit decisions require guidance from faculty or specialists. Ethical constraints apply because ZNERV focuses on major depressive disorder and must be presented as an engineering prototype without unsupported claims of clinical effectiveness. Our predetermined 21-minute session includes a 30-second ramp-up, 20-minute stimulation interval, and 30-second ramp-down, with device control enforcing timing independently of the frontend countdown. Safety requirements create a trade-off with our $750 budget, so output monitoring and fault protection must take priority over optional features and enclosure refinements. Social constraints apply because our team contract requires a device that is easy for both users and doctors to operate. A single predetermined setting with Start and Stop controls, scalable text, clear labels, and status messages that do not rely only on color will support accessible use. Session history will distinguish completed and interrupted runs and display interruption reasons so users and clinicians can understand each session’s outcome.
